Research

Two pilot studies, one problem

Detectors perform well on known data but fail on new samples. The two studies below examine this problem in audio and in symbolic music.

Study 1 · Audio

AI Music Detection on Unseen Generators

Independent study · 2026 · FakeMusicCaps · PyTorch

Generative models evolve quickly; a detector’s practical value depends on whether it recognizes models it has never seen. This study trains detectors on real music and on output from four text-to-music models, holds out a fifth for testing, and rotates through all five. Detectors operate on log-mel spectrograms, using logistic regression and a small CNN, with two methods tested to reduce reliance on each model’s overall spectral envelope.

0.975Balanced accuracy of the CNN on seen generators
0.50Detection rate on unseen generators
0.21AUC of a spectral-statistics detector on MusicLDM, below chance
  • Data. 2,856 real clips (MusicCaps) and 27,605 AI clips from five text-to-music models, 10 s each at 16 kHz. Train–test split by prompt to prevent content leakage.
  • Evaluation. Each generator held out in turn, simulating the release of a new model on a streaming platform.
  • No universal fix. Removing the spectral envelope raises MusicLDM AUC from 0.69 to 0.90 but lowers performance on MusicGen and Stable Audio Open.
  • Architecture dependence. Models decoding through a mel-spectrogram VAE and HiFi-GAN transfer to one another; MusicGen and Stable Audio Open remain difficult (detection 0.18–0.34).

Limitations: 52% of real MusicCaps clips retrievable; real audio lossy (YouTube), AI audio lossless; 3 seeds; five open-source models only.

Study 2 · Symbolic music

Testing a Beethoven Classifier on Recombined Imitations

Reproduction and extension of Galajda & Hua (IEEE MIPR 2024) · 2026 · MAESTRO v3 · PyTorch

AI systems can imitate a composer by splicing fragments of the composer’s own works. Using Beethoven as a case study, this study reproduces a published Beethoven classifier (Galajda & Hua, 2024) on the MAESTRO piano dataset, tests it on imitations spliced from real bars of different works, and trains a detector based solely on bar-to-bar transitions.

0.96AUC of the reproduced classifier, stable under stricter data splits
83–92%Spliced imitations classified as Beethoven (originals: 83%)
0.94–0.96AUC of the transition detector, spliced vs. original
  • Reproduction. Two-layer GRU on 27 features per 2-second bar, following Galajda & Hua (2024). AUC remains 0.96 under work- and year-based splits.
  • Splicing test. Cross-work splicing does not lower the score; splicing other composers’ music raises its Beethoven rate from 8% to 25%.
  • Transition features. Focus on bar boundaries: key change, melodic leap, density shift, interrupted sustain. These features reliably identify spliced segments.
  • Generalization gap. Trained on random splices only, the detector falls to AUC 0.73 on selected smooth splices; trained the other way, to 0.56. Selected splices are overly smooth: mean melodic leap of 1.6 semitones versus 9.1 in Beethoven.
